TASK 02

Organic vs Inorganic Semiconductor Selection

Charge Transport Dynamics & Exciton Physics

Examine how crystal symmetry, electronic delocalization, and Coulombic dielectric screening dictate whether carriers propagate as Bloch wavepackets or localized polarons hopping between molecular orbitals.

INORGANIC CRYSTALLINE REGIME (Si / GaAs)
  • • High Dielectric Constant ($\varepsilon_r \approx 11.7 - 12.9$): Strong screening creates weakly bound Wannier-Mott excitons ($E_b \approx 3.8 - 4.1\text{ meV}$).
  • • Bloch Wave Delocalization: Electron mobility $\mu_e > 1000\text{ cm}^2/\text{V}\cdot\text{s}$, permitting thick absorber layers ($> 2\text{ }\mu\text{m}$) without carrier starvation.
  • • Thermal Stability: Invariant performance up to $> 150^\circ\text{C}$; requires high energy epitaxy / cleanroom vacuum fabrication.
ORGANIC CONJUGATED REGIME (Alq3 / PPV)
  • • Low Dielectric Constant ($\varepsilon_r \approx 3.0 - 3.8$): Weak screening binds Frenkel excitons tightly ($E_b \approx 300 - 800\text{ meV}$), requiring donor-acceptor interfaces to split.
  • • Gaussian Disorder Hopping: Carrier mobility $\mu \sim 10^{-4}\text{ cm}^2/\text{V}\cdot\text{s}$; active layer thickness strictly constrained to $< 100\text{ nm}$.
  • • Mechanical Flexibility: Soluble printing, bend radius $< 2\text{ mm}$, continuous roll-to-roll scaling at $< 120^\circ\text{C}$.
