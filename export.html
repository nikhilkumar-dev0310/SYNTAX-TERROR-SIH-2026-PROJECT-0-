Executive Summary & Dual Run Comparison

Method: DFT-B3LYP / Drift-Diffusion Coupled

This investigation evaluates the complementary transport dynamics of optoelectronic devices across two divergent energy paradigms: direct radiative recombination within a narrow-band organic emitter (Run 01: Alq3 Green OLED) versus photo-carrier collection and dissociation across a crystalline tandem interface (Run 02: GaAs / Metal-Halide Perovskite Absorber). The simulation demonstrates how exciton binding energy thresholds govern whether optical interactions require dielectric screening or molecular polaron hopping.

§ 03

Student Engineering Justifications & Tradeoff Arguments

Argument A Barrier Integrity

Coating Selection Rationale: Flexible Encapsulation vs Anti-Reflective (ARC)

"For the Alq3 configuration, the catastrophic degradation vector is ambient H₂O/O₂ induced pinhole quenching of the low-work-function magnesium cathode. Therefore, priority was allocated to an atomic layer deposited (ALD) Al₂O₃/organic multilayer flexible nanolaminate with a water vapor transmission rate (WVTR) below 10⁻⁶ g/m²/day, rather than maximizing surface optical throughput. In the GaAs tandem cell, optical reflections account for over 31% primary loss without surface texturing; here, a dual-layer MgF₂/TiO₂ broadband anti-reflective coating was mandated to match the AM1.5 solar terrestrial flux profile."
Verified Parameter: WVTR < 10⁻⁶ Confidence Score: 98%
Argument B Charge Transport

Organic vs. Inorganic Electronic Tradeoff Analysis

"The starkest constraint lies in carrier mobility: crystalline GaAs exhibits bulk electron mobility of 8,500 cm²/V·s through continuous delocalized conduction bands. Conversely, Alq3 relies on thermally activated polaron hopping between discrete localized molecular orbitals, suppressing mobility to ~1.4 × 10⁻⁴ cm²/V·s. Consequently, active layer thickness in Alq3 is constrained to < 100 nm to mitigate space-charge-limited current (SCLC) voltage drop, while the GaAs cell can exploit a 2.5 μm absorption path length without suffering severe collection drift losses."
Model: Mott-Gurney SCLC Tradeoff Verified
Argument C Spectral Matching

Energy Scenario Selection Justification: Shockley-Queisser Limit

"A single junction with Eg = 2.7 eV is unsuitable for terrestrial solar harvesting due to transmission of all photon wavelengths > 460 nm, resulting in a maximum theoretical thermodynamic efficiency of < 8.2%. Placing GaAs (Eg = 1.42 eV) proximate to the optimal single-junction band gap (1.34 eV) allows broad spectral utilization. Pairing it with a 1.55 eV perovskite in a tandem architecture circumvents the single-junction thermalization penalty, elevating theoretical maximum conversion efficiency beyond 33.7%."
Condition: AM1.5G 1000 W/m² Optimum Achieved
