Delocalized Band Transport

In crystalline semiconductors (Si, GaAs, InGaN), rigid spatial periodicity yields continuous valence and conduction energy bands. Electron-hole pairs possess tiny Coulombic binding ($E_b \approx 4\text{–}15\text{ meV}$), which readily dissociate spontaneously at room thermal energy ($k_B T \approx 25.9\text{ meV}$).

Localized Polaron Hopping

In molecular thin-films (Alq3, Poly-TPD, PCBM), weak van der Waals coupling and low dielectric constants ($\varepsilon_r \approx 3\text{–}4$) tightly confine electron-hole pairs to single monomer sites ($r \approx 0.8\text{ nm}$). Carriers travel via phonon-assisted, thermally activated tunneling between discrete HOMO and LUMO energy levels.

Engineering Parameter Inorganic Semiconductors (Si, GaAs, InP) Organic Semiconductors (Small Mol. / Polymers) Optoelectronic Design Consequence
Electronic Delocalization Broad 3D energy bands ($W > 2\text{ eV}$) Discrete HOMO/LUMO states ($W < 0.2\text{ eV}$) Inorganics achieve fast drift; organics depend on structural hopping order.
Exciton Binding ($E_b$) Weak: $3.8 \text{ to } 15\text{ meV}$ ($\varepsilon_r \approx 12$) Strong: $300 \text{ to } 800\text{ meV}$ ($\varepsilon_r \approx 3.5$) Organics require Donor-Acceptor interfaces to overcome the Onsager-Braun barrier.
Carrier Mobility ($\mu$) $100 \text{ to } 8,500\text{ cm}^2/\text{V}\cdot\text{s}$ $10^{-5} \text{ to } 1\text{ cm}^2/\text{V}\cdot\text{s}$ Organics require sub-100nm active layer thickness to prevent catastrophic RC time-lag.
Mechanical Compliancy Rigid, brittle crystalline wafer (fracture strain < 1%) Extremely flexible, rollable polymer (strain > 15%) Enables bendable displays, smart textiles, conformal aerospace arrays.
Fabrication Footprint High-temperature ($900\text{--}1400^\circ\text{C}$), vacuum litho Solution printing, slot-die, thermal evp ($< 150^\circ\text{C}$) Drastically reduced thermal energy budget & continuous roll-to-roll scaling.
